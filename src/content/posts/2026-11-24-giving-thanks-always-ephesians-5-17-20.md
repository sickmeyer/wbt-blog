# Giving Thanks Always: What Ephesians 5:20 Says Thanksgiving Really Means

The apostle Paul told the church at Ephesus to be "giving thanks always for all things." Once a year, this country sets aside a Thursday and tries to manage it for an afternoon.

I love that Thursday. Thanksgiving means a great deal to America and to her families. It is a time of togetherness, and in a lot of homes it is a picture of what a home ought to be. Everybody sits down together, there is fellowship around the table, and there is the canned, gelled cranberry sauce with the ridges still on it.

No? It has to be homemade? All right.

I understand that not every home looks like that. But many do, and I praise the Lord for them. For a lot of families it is a time of reminiscing, of telling again all the good times that have been shared. When you hear the word *Thanksgiving*, you probably think of baked turkey and baked ham and all the trimmings and the family coming over. There is nothing wrong with any of that.

But is that all there is to Thanksgiving?

In a spiritual way, what is Thanksgiving to you? Here is the passage I want you to sit with while you think about it:

> "Wherefore be ye not unwise, but understanding what the will of the Lord is. And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit; Speaking to yourselves in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord; Giving thanks always for all things unto God and the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ;" (Ephesians 5:17-20)

Notice where Paul puts thanksgiving. He ties it to understanding the will of the Lord and to being filled with the Spirit, which means the Holy Spirit. Giving thanks is not a seasonal mood. It is what God wants from you.

> "In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you." (1 Thessalonians 5:18)

## Giving thanks always depends on what you see

True thanksgiving is vision-based.

The more self-centered you are, the less you see the people around you. The less you see them, the less you appreciate them. It becomes about you and not about others. And if it's about you, it's not about Him.

I believe this is an encouraging message. I also believe it is a convicting one, because every one of us can improve on being thankful. I know I can.

## 1. Meaningful thanksgiving keeps heaven open

It keeps heaven open in three ways: through adoration, appreciation, and acclamation.

What I mean by that is simple. God is never offended when you praise Him from the heart and thank Him. **The level of your thankfulness is directly proportional to how much you adore and appreciate God for who He is and what He has done.**

Look at the four and twenty elders in Revelation:

> "And when those beasts give glory and honour and thanks to him that sat on the throne, who liveth for ever and ever, The four and twenty elders fall down before him that sat on the throne, and worship him that liveth for ever and ever, and cast their crowns before the throne, saying, Thou art worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honour and power: for thou hast created all things, and for thy pleasure they are and were created." (Revelation 4:9-11)

I believe those beasts are the seraphim that Isaiah saw. There isn't room here to go through all my reasons, but look at what the prophet saw:

> "In the year that king Uzziah died I saw also the LORD sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up, and his train filled the temple. Above it stood the seraphims: each one had six wings; with twain he covered his face, and with twain he covered his feet, and with twain he did fly. And one cried unto another, and said, Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory." (Isaiah 6:1-3)

They cried *Holy, holy, holy*, and it went on and on. I believe John saw the same creatures, still giving glory and honour and thanks. Every time they do, the elders fall down. It happens again in the next chapter, and then again:

> "And every creature which is in heaven, and on the earth, and under the earth, and such as are in the sea, and all that are in them, heard I saying, Blessing, and honour, and glory, and power, be unto him that sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb for ever and ever. And the four beasts said, Amen. And the four and twenty elders fell down and worshipped him that liveth for ever and ever." (Revelation 5:13-14)

Notice something about those elders. In one place after another, they keep falling down. They keep humbling themselves in the sight of the Lord, casting their crowns before Him and giving Him the glory. They never get used to Him.

## The woman with the alabaster box saw what Simon missed

Luke tells about a woman who came into a Pharisee's house carrying an alabaster box of ointment.

> "And stood at his feet behind him weeping, and began to wash his feet with tears, and did wipe them with the hairs of her head, and kissed his feet, and anointed them with the ointment." (Luke 7:38)

She proved her thankfulness by the depth of her worship. Her heart was prepared to worship Him and ready to fall before Him. It was not about her. It was all about Him.

You know the rest of the story. She did this in the house of Simon the Pharisee, and Simon sat there accusing the Lord in his heart:

> "Now when the Pharisee which had bidden him saw it, he spake within himself, saying, This man, if he were a prophet, would have known who and what manner of woman this is that toucheth him: for she is a sinner." (Luke 7:39)

Here is what convicts me about that. A lot of times Christians lose appreciation for the people around them simply because they look a little different, dress a little different, or act a little different. Maybe they don't smell right. Maybe they don't look right. And we have zero appreciation for them.

Yet even the Pharisees had to admit what the Lord was doing:

> "And when the Pharisees saw it, they said unto his disciples, Why eateth your Master with publicans and sinners?" (Matthew 9:11)

Every one of us is a sinner. If you happen to be saved, you are a sinner saved by grace. That is the only difference between you and the person you would rather not sit next to.

You are important to God. But **the more we matter to ourselves, the less God matters to us.**

## His praise shall continually be in my mouth

The second part is appreciation, our praise to His name.

> "I will bless the LORD at all times: his praise shall continually be in my mouth." (Psalms 34:1)

David wrote that after the day he changed his behavior before Abimelech. He was among the Philistines and afraid for his life, so he pretended to be insane. He foamed at the mouth and carried on so they would not kill him. He never should have been in that situation. It was his own poor choice. But God delivered him anyway, and David came out of it blessing the LORD. You and I have just as much to praise Him for.

> "I will praise thee for ever, because thou hast done it: and I will wait on thy name; for it is good before thy saints." (Psalms 52:9)

Do you know what a saint is? It is a saved person, a saved sinner. Not someone crowned by Rome. If you are saved, that verse is talking about you.

> "I will praise the name of God with a song, and will magnify him with thanksgiving. This also shall please the LORD better than an ox or bullock that hath horns and hoofs. The humble shall see this, and be glad: and your heart shall live that seek God." (Psalms 69:30-32)

Look at that phrase: *magnify him with thanksgiving*. God is magnified through the giving of thanks. It pleases Him better than an ox or a bullock, better than any animal sacrifice. Your praise means more to Him than all of that.

That's why I love the old song "Singing I Go." *Singing, I go along life's road, praising my Lord.* It is exactly what Paul told the Ephesians to do: "singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord."

> "I will sing of the mercies of the LORD for ever: with my mouth will I make known thy faithfulness to all generations." (Psalms 89:1)

Notice *LORD* in all capitals. When you see that in the Old Testament, I believe it is a pre-incarnate appearing of Christ. It is the name Jehovah, and that name pictures the arm of the Lord reaching down through a window of heaven, exposing the palm to the chisel end of a nail, and going back up through the window into heaven. The name itself pictures the death, burial, and resurrection of Christ. That is amazing when you stop to think about it.

## Come and hear what He hath done for my soul

The third part is acclamation. We sing about it in "How Great Thou Art." When Christ shall come with shout of acclamation, we are going to fall before Him. But you don't have to wait for that day to start.

This is my life's verse:

> "Come and hear, all ye that fear God, and I will declare what he hath done for my soul." (Psalms 66:16)

Talk with me long enough and I will declare what He has done for my soul. That's for sure.

> "I will give thee thanks in the great congregation: I will praise thee among much people." (Psalms 35:18)

That means you can't be shy about it.

I think of Brother Silcox, whom I consider my grandfather in the faith. One day he and I went to a restaurant in a hotel here in town, and it was crowded. He walked in, looked around the room, and said about this loud, "Well, praise the Lord! I'm saved, I'm on my way to heaven. Praise God for Jesus!" I started looking around at the people. Some of them said, "Amen."

He was not embarrassed by his King. He gave thanks in the great congregation, even when the congregation was a restaurant.

## 2. Thanksgiving is an opportunity to help, to heal, and to humble yourself

We live in a day of entitlement. Have you noticed? It doesn't matter which generation you belong to. Gas prices go up or something else happens, and others take a hike. It's survival. It's whatever benefits self.

We are a deeply divided nation. But when you get right down to the core of it, it's all about self. *What can I do to better me?*

I trust that is not you. I hope you have the attitude Christ would want you to have: *what can I do to help somebody else?*

It is amazing what a simple thank you will do for someone. Why is it so hard for people to say it? Somebody helps you out, and you walk off without a word. That's just bad manners. And it is really bad when a Christian doesn't appreciate what people do. Men, we don't have many gentlemen anymore. Open the door for your wife, and wait until she's in the car.

A thank you means the most to somebody who is having a bad day. Have you ever had lousy service at a restaurant? I've heard that the greatest insult you can leave a server is a penny on the table. A Christian should never do that. You don't know what is happening in that person's life. Maybe something terrible happened today. Maybe they don't like the way they're acting either, but they have to make ends meet, so there they are.

Why not leave a little extra? I know that goes against conventional thinking. You don't have to say, "You look like you're having a bad day." Don't state the obvious. Just be a blessing. If you knew what they were carrying, you might be just as testy. And before you say you would never be that way, remember that you may be tested on it quicker than you think.

## Do good to them which hate you

> "But I say unto you which hear, Love your enemies, do good to them which hate you, Bless them that curse you, and pray for them which despitefully use you." (Luke 6:27-28)

When was the last time you did good to somebody who hated you?

I am just as guilty as the next man on this one. If I notice somebody out there who hates the ground I walk on, I give him a wide berth. But that is not what the Lord said.

> "Therefore if thine enemy hunger, feed him; if he thirst, give him drink: for in so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire on his head." (Romans 12:20)

Some people read that and say, "That's all I have to do? Treat my enemy nice, and God will dump hot coals on his head?" No, that's not what it's saying. It means your enemy will not be able to handle kindness from someone he pictures as his enemy. It will tear him apart inside.

I am not saying everyone should be expected to do what I'm about to describe. But let me tell you about a mother whose family went camping. Her daughter was in a tent near a creek bed when a man came and took her away, violated her, and killed her. At his trial, that mother stood up and forgave him publicly.

He could not handle it. If my memory serves me right, he either tried to take his own life afterward or did. He could not understand how anyone could show that kind of love after the pain he had inflicted.

Now, I am not saying the state should forgive him. He most certainly should receive the death penalty and go away forever. But think about how that mother rose to the occasion. Remember the Lord on the cross, looking down at the men who had just crucified Him, with the Jewish high council standing there:

> "Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots." (Luke 23:34)

In Christ, anything is possible. All things are possible with God.

There is healing in it, too. I believe the victory for that mother was that the man no longer lived in her head rent free. **When you cannot forgive, you carry it, bitterness sets in, and it will destroy you from the inside out.**

> "And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you." (Ephesians 4:32)

## Heal the hurt at home

Not every home is *Leave It to Beaver*. I understand that. But I want to speak to the children first. Appreciate your parents. We are not perfect. Grandparents are not perfect. We make mistakes, and we need to own them. But thank the Lord for your mom and dad, for grandma and grandpa, however it works in your family. Thank them while you can. You don't want to live your life with regrets.

You might say, "Pastor, you don't know what my dad did. You don't know what my uncle did." You're absolutely right. I don't. Maybe there has been no remorse and no repentance on their part. But here is what I do know: for your own sake, you need to forgive from the heart and move forward. Nobody is saying you have to reconcile and act like nothing ever happened. But forgive, so you have victory in your own heart and that situation doesn't live in your head rent free.

Now, parents, thank you for being mom and dad. Maybe you didn't have the means the family across the street had, or the folks on the next farm over. Things can't buy happiness. What a child wants more than anything else is simple time with mom and dad.

Quality time.

We think we have to provide this and that for Joey or Suzy so they will appreciate us. You can't buy that. What you can do is show love. The neighbors have a TV and you don't? That's not the end of the world. Fold up a paper football and flick it back and forth across the kitchen table. If you've never done that, you are missing out.

And learn to say two words: *I'm sorry.* Anybody can say "I apologize." There is a certain professionalism in it, but it doesn't carry the warmth of a true "I'm sorry." Don't be afraid to say it.

## Never be too proud to be helped

Humble yourself in thanksgiving. You should never be ashamed of receiving help.

Men, if you see somebody who looks like he needs help, just go help. Don't ask, "Do you need help?" Any man with an ego is going to say, "No, no, I'm fine." He's not fine. His arms are quivering like everything, but he won't let his face show it. You know what I'm talking about.

So don't ask. Say, "Here, let me take that for you." If he says, "I've got it," help him anyway.

And if you see a lady struggling and you just keep right on walking, what in the world? My soul.

## So what does Thanksgiving mean to you?

Before long you will be sitting at a table somewhere, with cranberry sauce from a can or not. The turkey is fine. The family is a gift. But Paul did not tell you to give thanks on a Thursday in November. He said *giving thanks always for all things unto God and the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.*

So look up. Fall down before Him like those elders. Open your mouth and tell somebody what He has done for your soul. Thank the waitress. Do good to the one who hates you. Forgive the one who hurt you. Tell your children you're sorry. Let somebody carry the load.

What does Thanksgiving mean to you? Who will you thank first? Start today.
