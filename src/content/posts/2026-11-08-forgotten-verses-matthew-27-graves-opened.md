# The Forgotten Verses of Matthew 27: When the Graves Were Opened

The sun goes dark over Jerusalem at noon. For three hours a darkness sits on the land so thick you could almost feel it. In the middle of it, outside the city wall, a Man hangs on a cross.

Then He cries out, and He dies. And Matthew, without so much as stopping for breath, tells you something that ought to stop you cold:

> "And the graves were opened; and many bodies of the saints which slept arose, And came out of the graves after his resurrection, and went into the holy city, and appeared unto many." (Matthew 27:52-53)

Dead people walked into Jerusalem, and the people who saw them knew who they were.

Only Matthew records it. I'll be honest with you: for years I read those two verses just to get through them. I preached the verses before them and the verses after them, and I skipped right over the middle. I don't believe I'm the only preacher who has done that.

But I have always believed that nothing in the Word of God is without significance. If God put it there, there is a reason it is there.

So what is the reason? And here is the question you have every right to ask: what does it have to do with you?

## These forgotten verses cut two ways

It has everything to do with you, and it cuts in two directions.

If you have been born again, these forgotten verses of Matthew 27 give you a preview. They show you the resurrection unto life that is waiting for you. It is hard to comprehend what that is going to be like. To call it awesome doesn't do it justice.

If you are not saved, these same verses are a warning. The Lord Jesus said there are two resurrections, not one:

> "Marvel not at this: for the hour is coming, in the which all that are in the graves shall hear his voice, And shall come forth; they that have done good, unto the resurrection of life; and they that have done evil, unto the resurrection of damnation." (John 5:28-29)

Now, who are "they that have done good"? The Bible is plain that it isn't any of us on our own:

> "They are all gone out of the way, they are together become unprofitable; there is none that doeth good, no, not one." (Romans 3:12)
> "For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;" (Romans 3:23)

God is good. The Lord Jesus Christ is good. The ones who have "done good" are those who have repented and trusted Christ. They are not good of themselves. They are good because of Him.

And you cannot treat the Bible like an all-you-can-eat buffet, taking the parts you like and leaving the rest on the line. If these verses about the saints rising are true, and they are, then the danger the lost person is in is just as true.

## Nothing rises until the Lamb dies

Before we get to the saints, we have to stand at the cross. Without what happens there, not one of those graves opens.

> "Now from the sixth hour there was darkness over all the land unto the ninth hour. And about the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, saying, Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani? that is to say, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?" (Matthew 27:45-46)

He had been on that cross since the third hour, nine in the morning. From noon until three in the afternoon, the darkness covered everything. In that darkness the Father turned His back on the Son, because the Son was becoming sin for us. The Lamb of God was shedding His perfect blood for everybody from Adam and Eve until now and beyond.

Isaiah saw it seven centuries before it happened. This is the chapter of His suffering in our place:

> "He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief: and we hid as it were our faces from him; he was despised, and we esteemed him not. Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted. But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all." (Isaiah 53:3-6)

You may be walking wounded right now. But nobody was ever wounded worse than our Lord. God is able to make a way of escape for you and me. **There was no way of escape for Him.** He had to finish the mission, all the way to the end of the cross, and we will be rejoicing for all eternity that He did.

> "For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him." (2 Corinthians 5:21)
> "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life." (John 3:16)

John tells us how the end came:

> "After this, Jesus knowing that all things were now accomplished, that the scripture might be fulfilled, saith, I thirst. Now there was set a vessel full of vinegar: and they filled a spunge with vinegar, and put it upon hyssop, and put it to his mouth. When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost." (John 19:28-30)

Hyssop. I have no doubt our Lord drew the connection the moment that branch touched His lips. Hyssop was what Israel used on Passover night in Egypt to put the blood on the doorway, across the top and down both sides, a picture of the cross. Then He said, "It is finished."

I get a lump in my throat every time I come to that.

## Seven events, joined by one little word

From that moment, Matthew fires off seven events in rapid succession. He ties them together with the word *and*, and he does not put down a period until he is through.

> "Jesus, when he had cried again with a loud voice, yielded up the ghost. And, behold, the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom; and the earth did quake, and the rocks rent;" (Matthew 27:50-51)

The veil was torn. The earth shook. The rocks split. The graves opened. The saints arose, came out, went into the city, and appeared unto many.

Notice that none of it happened on its own. That veil did not tear itself, and it tore from the top down, because God tore it. The earth did not decide to quake. Archaeologists have found evidence of a major earthquake in Jerusalem around AD 33, just like the Bible says. The tombs did not open themselves. **Every one of those events was the supernatural power of Almighty God.**

And while we are here: do you know what a saint is? It is not someone the Vatican has declared to be a saint. A saint is simply a saved sinner.

## In Jerusalem, a tomb was a womb

To see this the way the people of that day saw it, you have to picture Jerusalem's graves.

They were not flat plots in the ground like ours. Families hired stonemasons to cut chambers into the limestone hillsides. Inside, the masons chiseled long recesses into the walls, each just long enough to hold a body. A great rolling stone sealed the entrance. The slopes of the Mount of Olives and the Kidron Valley were dotted with these tombs. Some belonged to wealthy families. Some held generations of the faithful dead.

Here is something I found fascinating. The Hebrew word for grave is *kever*, and it was also used as a word for the womb. In Jewish thinking, the grave was not the final end. It was a holding place, a womb where the body waited to be brought forth again.

You and I tend to look at the grave as the end. They saw it as a waiting room.

For the believer, that is exactly right. When you die, your soul does not wait in that grave:

> "We are confident, I say, and willing rather to be absent from the body, and to be present with the Lord." (2 Corinthians 5:8)

Your body waits. And it doesn't matter whether it was buried or cremated or lost at sea. God said:

> "In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread, till thou return unto the ground; for out of it wast thou taken: for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return." (Genesis 3:19)

The God who made you out of dust has no trouble putting the dust back together.

## Christ had to come out first

Look carefully at the wording. The graves opened when He died, but the saints did not come out until *after his resurrection*. That is not a detail to skip. It had to be that way.

> "But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept. For since by man came death, by man came also the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive. But every man in his own order: Christ the firstfruits; afterward they that are Christ’s at his coming." (1 Corinthians 15:20-23)

God always wanted the firstfruits. When He blessed Israel's crops, He said the first portion of that harvest belongs to Me.

You may ask, "What about Lazarus?" Lazarus was raised, bless his heart, but he died twice. Christ is the first to rise from the dead never to die again. He is the firstfruits of the resurrection, so He had to come out of the grave before anybody else would.

## Who were these saints?

The Bible does not tell us who they were. It says *many*, and many means more than a handful. Some believe they were believers who had died recently. The early church fathers believed they were Old Testament saints, perhaps dead for centuries.

What we do know is this: they were not ghosts. They were recognizable. They "appeared unto many," and people knew them. The word Matthew uses for their appearing is the same word used for the risen Christ appearing to His followers.

So here is what I think happened. Christ rose from the dead and entered the city, and the risen saints followed Him in.

That is my opinion. I don't have a verse to back it up, so don't quote me on it.

## Ezekiel saw it coming centuries before

Some five hundred years earlier, God gave a prophet a vision.

> "The hand of the LORD was upon me, and carried me out in the spirit of the LORD, and set me down in the midst of the valley which was full of bones, And caused me to pass by them round about: and, behold, there were very many in the open valley; and, lo, they were very dry. And he said unto me, Son of man, can these bones live? And I answered, O Lord GOD, thou knowest." (Ezekiel 37:1-3)
> "So I prophesied as I was commanded: and as I prophesied, there was a noise, and behold a shaking, and the bones came together, bone to his bone. And when I beheld, lo, the sinews and the flesh came up upon them, and the skin covered them above: but there was no breath in them." (Ezekiel 37:7-8)
> "Then said he unto me, Prophesy unto the wind, prophesy, son of man, and say to the wind, Thus saith the Lord GOD; Come from the four winds, O breath, and breathe upon these slain, that they may live. So I prophesied as he commanded me, and the breath came into them, and they lived, and stood up upon their feet, an exceeding great army." (Ezekiel 37:9-10)

Notice the order. The bones come together first, then the sinews and the flesh and the skin. Then, last of all, the breath.

I believe Ezekiel 37 is God's preview of Matthew 27. Think about those tombs during the days between the cross and the empty grave. The seals were already broken. I believe God was doing what Ezekiel saw. He was bringing bone to bone, restoring flesh, covering it with skin. Then, once Christ rose, He breathed life into them. When those saints walked into the city, they did not come out mangled. They looked like themselves, only now in a glorified state.

You know how a movie has a preview, to whet your appetite for what's coming? Ezekiel's valley was the preview. The tombs of Jerusalem were the first showing. And the main event is still coming.

When Jesus raised Lazarus, it was one man, four days dead. Here were many, some likely dead for centuries. **The same power that raised Lazarus, that can heal anyone of anything, that can lift the wounded in body and mind, opened those graves.**

Some will ask why only Matthew recorded it. That does not mean it didn't happen. Matthew wrote his Gospel most of all to Israel. The scholars of his day had long understood Ezekiel's vision as God's promise to raise His people from the dead, and they would have made the connection immediately. Outside of Scripture, the early church fathers, including Polycarp, a disciple of the apostle John, mention that many saints arose after Christ's resurrection. But Matthew needed no help. He did not write it with a shrug. He wrote it as fact, because it was fact.

Here is how I see it. Christ rising from the dead was proof that the Father had accepted His sacrifice. The torn veil said the way to the Father is open. And those saints walking into the holy city were the receipt.

## What the preview promises you

Now bring it home to yourself.

> "For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first: Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord." (1 Thessalonians 4:16-17)
> "And not only they, but ourselves also, which have the firstfruits of the Spirit, even we ourselves groan within ourselves, waiting for the adoption, to wit, the redemption of our body." (Romans 8:23)
> "Beloved, now are we the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is." (1 John 3:2)

Every believer you have laid in the ground, buried or cremated, will hear Him say *arise*. The graves will open, the bodies will be raised and reunited with souls that are already saved, and they will go into the holy city. There will be a new heaven and a new earth, and the redeemed will go in and out of that city.

Will it be out of this world? Absolutely.

## The resurrection nobody wants to talk about

As exciting as all that is, look at what Matthew did *not* say.

He did not say the bodies of the sinners arose. He said the bodies of the saints arose. God gave life to the ones who had life in Christ. The lost are raised later, at the great white throne. They are not raised to glory. They are raised to judgment.

> "For as the new heavens and the new earth, which I will make, shall remain before me, saith the LORD, so shall your seed and your name remain. And it shall come to pass, that from one new moon to another, and from one sabbath to another, shall all flesh come to worship before me, saith the LORD. And they shall go forth, and look upon the carcases of the men that have transgressed against me: for their worm shall not die, neither shall their fire be quenched; and they shall be an abhorring unto all flesh." (Isaiah 66:22-24)

Al Lacy, a Baptist preacher from years gone by, wrote a book on that passage. He believed the bodies of the lost would take on the appearance of a worm, an ugly, abhorrent thing. Is that how it will be? I don't know.

But I know God calls it a worm that does not die, and a fire that is not quenched.

If the preview of the saints was real, this is real too.

## Which resurrection will be yours?

> "And as it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment:" (Hebrews 9:27)

The same voice that called those saints out of their tombs will call you. Every grave is going to answer Him. The only question is which resurrection you come out to.

You do not get to the resurrection of life by being good, because none of us are. You get there the way every one of those saints got there. You repent of your sin and trust the Lord Jesus Christ, the One who was made sin for you and rose again the third day.

> "For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved." (Romans 10:13)
> "(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)" (2 Corinthians 6:2)

Those graves outside Jerusalem opened once. Yours will open too.

He will call your name.

Which way will you come forth?
