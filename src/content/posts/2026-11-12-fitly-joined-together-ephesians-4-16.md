# A Fitly Joined Body: Why Every Member Matters (Ephesians 4:16)

Years ago I got up at three in the morning and walked through the house barefoot. I like to walk through the house barefoot. That night somebody had left a metal scooter lying in the middle of the floor, and my little toe found it.

I'll only say this: the one that scooter belonged to was awake not long after I was.

Isn't it funny how one little toe can make every part of your body hurt? Nobody thinks about the little toe. Nobody thanks God for it in the morning. But hurt it once, and the whole body knows about it.

That is exactly what Paul was describing when he wrote to the church at Ephesus:

> "From whom the whole body fitly joined together and compacted by that which every joint supplieth, according to the effectual working in the measure of every part, maketh increase of the body unto the edifying of itself in love." (Ephesians 4:16)

Maybe you've sat in church and thought, *I don't really do anything. I come in, I warm a pew, I go home.* I want to encourage you. There is more you can do than you think, and you're already doing more of it than you realize.

The question is not whether the body needs you. The question is what your joint is supplying.

## The church is the thing the Lord loves most

There is one thing in the Bible that is most precious to the Lord, and that is His church.

The church is not a building of brick and mortar. It is an assembly of people who have been converted to Christ and scripturally baptized, which means by immersion and under proper authority. It is autonomous in its operation, local and visible, united by covenant in the Lord Jesus Christ and His Word, and empowered and enabled by the Holy Spirit. Christ is the head of that church, which is His body.

That's why He gave His churches pastors in the first place:

> "And he gave some, apostles; and some, prophets; and some, evangelists; and some, pastors and teachers; For the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ: Till we all come in the unity of the faith, and of the knowledge of the Son of God, unto a perfect man, unto the measure of the stature of the fulness of Christ:" (Ephesians 4:11-13)

Listen to the Lord Jesus praying to His Father:

> "I have glorified thee on the earth: I have finished the work which thou gavest me to do." (John 17:4)

He was not talking about His finished work on the cross. That hadn't happened yet, and you can't call something finished before it happens. He was talking about the constituting of His very first church, which began with the calling out of the twelve apostles.

Judas Iscariot would later be excluded from that body. The great commission was given to the eleven just before the Lord ascended back to heaven. From that day forward, the success of His churches has depended on each member faithfully doing his part.

Our Lord loves His churches above all else. He gave Himself for them.

> "Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;" (Ephesians 5:25)

So here is the question I keep coming back to. **Why are true churches today floundering instead of flourishing?**

Perhaps it's because the body is sick. Certain members aren't functioning the way they should.

You know how it works when you get sick. Your body kicks in its defenses: the fever, the antibodies, all that wonderful stuff. You feel lousy, and here's the funny thing: the worse you feel, the harder your body is fighting. Then you recover and come out stronger.

A broken arm is another matter. That isn't a three-day virus. It's six weeks, twelve weeks, depending on how bad the break is, and the whole body is affected the entire time.

In a church, every member is significant. Every member matters to how that body functions.

## 1. Christ is the head, so the foundation must be sure

> "But speaking the truth in love, may grow up into him in all things, which is the head, even Christ:" (Ephesians 4:15)
> "And hath put all things under his feet, and gave him to be the head over all things to the church, Which is his body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all." (Ephesians 1:22-23)

Everything rises and falls on what a church is anchored to.

If you've ever been out on a lake fishing, you know what an anchor does. You find a good fishing hole and drop anchor. The boat may drift a little and swing around some, but it's not going to leave that spot. It's the same as the house built on the rock. The winds may blow and the storms may rise, but it will not fall, because it's founded on the rock and not the sand.

If a church is anchored in Christ, He is the head of that body. And the head is the part that sends life, drive, and motion to every other part.

What happens to a body if the head gets cut off? It's done. It's lifeless.

The Lord is the source of life, energy, and increase in His church, specifically through the Holy Spirit, the Comforter. Every member draws its vigor and its strength from Him, and every joint contributes to the healthy action of the whole.

## The blood and the Spirit keep the body sealed

Speaking the truth in love means leaving the flesh out of the message. The Spirit launches it, aims it, and drives it home.

> "For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart." (Hebrews 4:12)

As preachers, we don't have to help the Lord apply His Word. He does it perfectly, every time. He doesn't need my assistance.

The church He applies it to was bought with blood:

> "Take heed therefore unto yourselves, and to all the flock, over the which the Holy Ghost hath made you overseers, to feed the church of God, which he hath purchased with his own blood." (Acts 20:28)

And the Spirit is the one who brings you into it:

> "Wherefore I give you to understand, that no man speaking by the Spirit of God calleth Jesus accursed: and that no man can say that Jesus is the Lord, but by the Holy Ghost." (1 Corinthians 12:3)
> "For by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body, whether we be Jews or Gentiles, whether we be bond or free; and have been all made to drink into one Spirit." (1 Corinthians 12:13)

That verse is one of the most abused and twisted in the Bible. People use it to say the church started at Pentecost, or to teach some separate Holy Spirit baptism. In context it says this: the same Spirit who leads you to be saved will lead you to join a true church by water baptism.

Think about Noah:

> "Make thee an ark of gopher wood; rooms shalt thou make in the ark, and shalt pitch it within and without with pitch." (Genesis 6:14)

Pitch is a tar-like substance, bitumen, that waterproofed the ark. And the Hebrew word behind it carries the idea of a covering. It is the same idea as atonement. Isn't that something?

I had a metal minnow bucket that kept leaking. I tried silicone. Then I took it all apart and went over it with Flex Seal. It's black, like tar, so I can honestly say I put pitch on the inside of my minnow bucket. It hasn't leaked a drop since.

God's people are sealed within and without too. We're sealed by the blood of Christ and by the indwelling Holy Spirit:

> "In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace;" (Ephesians 1:7)
> "In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise, Which is the earnest of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, unto the praise of his glory." (Ephesians 1:13-14)

*Earnest* is down money, like a deposit on a purchase agreement. God has put His down payment on you.

And you will never spring a leak.

The church was formed during Christ's earthly ministry, and it came fully into force with His death and resurrection, because a testament is not in force until the death of the testator. John the Baptist bridged the gap. He prepared the materials the Lord Jesus would use to form His first church, just as David prepared the materials for Solomon's temple.

## 2. God set you in the body exactly where He wanted you

> "But now hath God set the members every one of them in the body, as it hath pleased him." (1 Corinthians 12:18)

Joining a true church is a very serious matter. Whether you came by letter, by statement of like faith and practice, or by baptism, the Lord is the one who set you there. He put you exactly where you can benefit the body and bear the most fruit.

> "This is the LORD’s doing; it is marvellous in our eyes." (Psalms 118:23)
> "I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman. Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away: and every branch that beareth fruit, he purgeth it, that it may bring forth more fruit." (John 15:1-2)

Sometimes the purging comes through a trial or an affliction. God takes away more of us so that more of Him can work. The other side of it is sobering. A branch that absolutely refuses to bear fruit, He cuts away.

## Fitly joined: what every joint supplieth

*Fitly joined* means interlocking, made by design to be inseparable.

Think of Lego. A good piece doesn't just sit there. You have to press it, and it snaps. Once it's together, it holds. That's what members of a church do for one another.

No member is more important than another. It is a horrible thing when a church has its favorites and its elites. God chose the foolish and the weak things of this world to confound the wise. And if you think yourself to be something when you are nothing, you deceive yourself.

Then there's *compacted*. Do you remember trash compactors? We had one when I was a boy. I loved it when Mom turned it on and you could hear those glass two-liter bottles break. It's amazing what that thing could pack down.

*Compacted* means knit together into one whole. We aren't a collection of separate parts. We are one body.

*Every joint supplieth* means each joint connects one part to another and passes nourishment along. So when you come to church, check your attitude at the door. If your wife says you woke up on the wrong side of the bed, get on the right side of it before you walk in. Misery loves company, and you don't want to be a drag on the rest of the body.

When I see somebody walk in wearing an upside-down smile, I say, "Good to see you. You're here now." This is the day the Lord made, and we're going to rejoice and be glad in it.

Every member has something to contribute. A young couple in our church has had my wife and me over for fellowship more than once. That's the gift of hospitality, and I enjoyed it so much that we put out a sign-up sheet for others to host the church on a Sunday afternoon. I told them if nobody signed up, we'd just come to their house every other month. You should have seen the young man's face. I let him off the hook. My wife and I are signing up ourselves.

**There is no such thing as a spectator in a true church.** If you purpose in your heart to be a pew warmer, you are affecting the rest of the body.

Here's how you know. When the church gathers, everybody knows who's missing, and it affects us, even if only for a moment. The body is only fully assembled when all its members are present and in unity. If you could be in your seat and you aren't, you are missed.

> "And if they were all one member, where were the body? But now are they many members, yet but one body. And the eye cannot say unto the hand, I have no need of thee: nor again the head to the feet, I have no need of you. Nay, much more those members of the body, which seem to be more feeble, are necessary: And those members of the body, which we think to be less honourable, upon these we bestow more abundant honour; and our uncomely parts have more abundant comeliness." (1 Corinthians 12:19-23)

Drive across a few state lines and you'll find people are different everywhere. We have Iowans, South Dakotans, and North Dakotans visiting. Somehow this nation pulls all those backgrounds together as one nation under God. How much more should a church, through the precious blood of Christ?

The flesh will always want to say its own background is a little superior. We all have opinions, and there's nothing wrong with voicing one. But when all the opinions are in, God's is the one that counts.

## "But how do I really matter?"

Somebody reading this is asking that question. All the lighthearted things aside, you may be thinking it deep down: *I don't know my Bible very well. How do I really matter here?*

Let me say this from my heart. **No member who is faithfully serving the Lord is useless.**

There is another side to that coin. By design, no member should have to shoulder extra burden to pick up the slack for others.

I'm not only talking about finances, though that's part of it. I'm talking about yielding yourself to the Holy Spirit:

> "I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service." (Romans 12:1)

What does that look like on a Sunday? Others before self. Don't wait for somebody to come shake your hand. Go shake theirs. Don't wait for somebody to ask how you're doing. Go ask them.

## God strengthens the faithful when they are faint

Plenty of men have had both knees replaced, and they'll tell you it's a blessing. They'll also tell you there's nothing like the original. You wouldn't want God to replace you with a substitute either.

Paul says the body increases "according to the effectual working in the measure of every part." Each member contributes in proportion to his power and ability. Here's the key: **the more you lean on the Lord, the more you can do.**

Serve Him in your own strength and you'll wear out fast. Serve Him in His strength and He empowers you, enables you, and helps you flourish, not for your glory but for His.

Look at Gideon:

> "And Gideon came to Jordan, and passed over, he, and the three hundred men that were with him, faint, yet pursuing them. And he said unto the men of Succoth, Give, I pray you, loaves of bread unto the people that follow me; for they be faint, and I am pursuing after Zebah and Zalmunna, kings of Midian." (Judges 8:4-5)

Were they tired? Yes. Should Succoth have fed them? Yes, and Succoth was judged for refusing. But Gideon's men did not throw in the towel. They didn't know where the strength would come from, and God gave them enough to finish the task.

When a member isn't participating and another picks up the slack, the one sitting out misses the blessing. The one carrying the load gets a double blessing, and he gets worn out too. But he puts a smile on his face and keeps going for the glory of God.

And at some point the Lord says rest:

> "And the apostles gathered themselves together unto Jesus, and told him all things, both what they had done, and what they had taught. And he said unto them, Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while: for there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat." (Mark 6:30-31)

On another occasion the seventy came back rejoicing that even the devils were subject to them. The Lord put the glory right back where it belonged:

> "Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven." (Luke 10:20)

Whatever we do, we do by the grace of God.

## 3. Fashioned after His example

The body "maketh increase... unto the edifying of itself in love." That increase comes as we fashion ourselves after the Lord Jesus:

> "So after he had washed their feet, and had taken his garments, and was set down again, he said unto them, Know ye what I have done to you? Ye call me Master and Lord: and ye say well; for so I am. If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another’s feet. For I have given you an example, that ye should do as I have done to you." (John 13:12-15)

It isn't about the foot washing. It's about the example: stooping to the level of service, putting others above yourself, and doing it in love.

> "But Jesus called them unto him, and said, Ye know that the princes of the Gentiles exercise dominion over them, and they that are great exercise authority upon them. But it shall not be so among you: but whosoever will be great among you, let him be your minister; And whosoever will be chief among you, let him be your servant: Even as the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many." (Matthew 20:25-28)

That's how a fitly joined body grows. No one is climbing over anyone. Every joint is serving the next one.

## What is your joint supplying?

Remember that little toe and that metal scooter. One small part, and the whole body felt it.

You are a member of a body that was bought with blood and sealed by the Spirit. God set you there on purpose. When you're missing, the body feels it. When you serve, it grows.

So I'll ask you plainly. What does your joint supply?

Are you nourishing the body, or draining it?

Shake the hand. Take the seat. Pick up the towel.

The body needs you.
