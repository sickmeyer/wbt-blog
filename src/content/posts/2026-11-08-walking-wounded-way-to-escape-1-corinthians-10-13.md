# The Walking Wounded: A Way to Escape That Ye May Be Able to Bear It

The sun comes up on a man who cannot walk straight.

He spent the night alone, wrestling until the breaking of the day, and he came out of it with a new name and a hip that would never work right again. For the rest of his life, every step he took had a hitch in it. His name was Jacob, and by morning God had made him Israel.

He walked away from that place wounded. He also walked away more useful to God than he had ever been.

You may know something about walking like that. Most of us carry something: a loss, a betrayal, a disappointment that never quite left. We keep functioning while we carry it. We are the walking wounded. And God has made us a promise about it in 1 Corinthians 10:13:

> "There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it." (1 Corinthians 10:13)

Notice what that verse does not promise. It does not say the trial will leave. It promises a way to escape, *that ye may be able to bear it.* That leaves you with a question only you can answer.

When the wound came, did you bear it as a victim, or as a victor?

## Count it all joy? The trial is producing something

> "My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience." (James 1:2-3)

Read that cold and it sounds like the words of somebody who isn't quite all there. Count it joy? But James was moved by the Holy Spirit of God to write it, and he tells you why. *Knowing this.* You already know it, because you have seen it in your own life. The trying of your faith *worketh*, which means it produces something.

And what has it produced in you?

Patience.

I'll confess I am not built for it. Two hours into a head cold and I'm done with it. Then the Lord says, you need a little more patience, so this trial is going to last a little longer than you'd like.

We want the quick fix. Even the soap operas got cut down to thirty minutes, because the problem has to be wrapped up before the half hour is over. Real life does not work that way. The trial may not leave right away, and most of the time it doesn't.

But if your response to the trial is what it should be, you will come out of it a better Christian than you were when you went in.

## Name the wound so you can see how you handled it

It helps to name these wounds. Naming them helps you recognize them, and it helps you see whether you handled them as a victim or as a victor. If you are saved, the Holy Spirit lives in you. As you read this list, He is going to take your mind back through the files, and you will find yourself asking: How did I do? What was my response?

**Sudden loss.** I think of Job. One servant runs in to say the oxen and donkeys are gone and the men tending them are dead. Before he finishes, another comes about the sheep, and those servants are dead too. Then the camels. And then the last messenger:

> "While he was yet speaking, there came also another, and said, Thy sons and thy daughters were eating and drinking wine in their eldest brother’s house: And, behold, there came a great wind from the wilderness, and smote the four corners of the house, and it fell upon the young men, and they are dead; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee." (Job 1:18-19)

That is sudden loss. It comes in other ways too. A marriage you thought was fine is suddenly over. Someone abandons you, which is another way of saying, I don't care for you anymore. A job disappears, and what you thought would be temporary goes on for a long while.

**Betrayal.** A friend betrays you, the buddy who was supposed to stick with you through thick and thin. A spouse betrays you, and I think that is about as terrible as it gets. In a church it is often a member who rises up against the pastor. In the military it is a leader who betrays his men. That's tough.

**Trauma.** The more I read about post-traumatic stress, the more convinced I am that all of us go through it. It is common to man. How we go through it is what can turn it into a disorder, and it doesn't have to become one. A young lady in a class I taught this past week told me she remembers the day her husband pulled a gun on her. Can you imagine? There is the trauma of violence, and of accidents that can leave a person disabled or paralyzed for life. There is the trauma of war. Men who came home from the Second World War often would not talk about it. They didn't want to live through it again.

## Disappointment destroyed the wisest counselor in Israel

Has anyone lived a whole life without being disappointed? About the only person who escapes it is the extreme pessimist, because he expected everything to go bad in the first place.

Ahithophel was regarded as the best counselor there ever was. King David followed his counsel. When Absalom rose up, both Ahithophel and Hushai gave him advice, and God turned the hearts of Absalom and the people to follow Hushai. Look at what Ahithophel did when he realized his counsel would not be followed:

> "And when Ahithophel saw that his counsel was not followed, he saddled his ass, and arose, and gat him home to his house, to his city, and put his household in order, and hanged himself, and died, and was buried in the sepulchre of his father." (2 Samuel 17:23)

That was his response to disappointment, all because his advice wasn't taken.

I bring him up for a reason. Some people pour everything into a business venture, and when it fails they cannot handle it, and they take their own lives. Not much is said about it, but it happens. Dreams that never come true leave a real wound.

So does failed ministry, and if you are saved, you are in ministry. You witness to someone again and again, and they die, and as far as you know they never got saved. That has affected me. All I can do is trust that maybe somebody else got to talk with them before the end.

So does a relationship that ends after you have given your heart to it. My wife and I have talked about this. I invested about four years of my life in a relationship, and then I got a letter from John Deere. Flip the name around and you'll know what kind it was. No, they weren't offering me a job. They were offering me the door.

The question is the same for every one of these: victim or victor?

**Chronic stress.** More people in this country live under long-term financial pressure than you would guess, and some of you are among them. Then there is caregiving that seems to never end. Caregiving is one of the most wonderful things you can do, because you are giving your life for someone else. But caring for a parent or a child year after year is a lot of stress. And chronic health problems keep becoming more common. We argue about the cause, but whatever the source, they are there. How are you going to respond?

I think of Joni Eareckson Tada, who has served the Lord most of her life unable to move from the neck down. She decided to be a victor through it. She decided to take it scripturally. What a concept. Some of us carry far less than that, and I'm one of them, and we can't help but complain.

**Spiritual wounds.** These are more common among Christian people than anybody talks about. People are leaving Baptist churches by the droves because of past legalism. If you have experienced it, you may still think about it to this day, and it can take you right out of service. Then there is hypocrisy. In my opinion nothing is more damaging than someone who says he is a Christian, maybe someone in leadership, living a lie. We can act like that wound isn't there, but it is.

Maybe the Holy Spirit has been helping you recognize yourself in this list. Maybe He is helping you admit that you've been the victim. It's time to stop. **You do not have to remain a victim.** The causes are many, but there is one Healer for all of them, and He can carry you through.

## Smiling while you bleed

Walking wounded people keep going while they carry pain. They hide their wounds behind smiles. They serve while they're hurting. Pastors preach while they're grieving. You parent or grandparent while you're broken. You worship while you carry the pain. And often we pretend everything is fine because we are afraid others will judge us.

Others pull away entirely. This church has been here going on twenty-seven years, and some who started with it are not here anymore. Some people withdraw because they are afraid of being vulnerable. It's sad.

But the danger does not stop at being a victim.

## What a wound becomes when you won't let it go

Ask the Holy Spirit to show you where you really are. Nothing will be gained from this unless you take it personally. When a victim holds on to his wound, it turns into something else, and Satan wants it to.

It turns to **bitterness.** And bitterness usually starts with a sentence I remember coming out of my own mouth: *I have a right to be bitter because of this.*

It turns to **cynicism**, the conviction that you can't trust anybody.

> "Put not your trust in princes, nor in the son of man, in whom there is no help." (Psalms 146:3)

That verse is telling you not to run to leaders for the solution to all your problems. It isn't going to happen there.

It turns to **isolation.** Why do you only watch the services on a livestream? If you are homebound and cannot be there, you are not forsaking anything. But if you are able-bodied and choose not to come, read this:

> "And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works: Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching." (Hebrews 10:24-25)

You cannot provoke people to love and good works when all you see of them is a picture on a screen.

It turns to **anger.** An angry Christian is one of the saddest things you will ever see, because the joy of the Lord is not showing at all. Eventually that anger turns on God: *Why don't You change this?* But God is under no obligation to do our bidding. We are supposed to serve Him, not the other way around.

And it turns to **coldness** toward spiritual things. It's easy to see that in somebody else. Be honest enough to ask whether it is in you.

## The thing Satan meant to destroy you

Do not think for a moment you are so unimportant that Satan doesn't want to destroy you.

> "The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly." (John 10:10)

But when a victor brings his wound to Christ, things get a lot simpler. The very thing Satan meant to destroy you can become the instrument God uses to help someone else.

> "Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God." (2 Corinthians 1:4)

**Your wound can become your ministry.** That is one of the reasons I'm writing this at all. On July 31st, my wife and I thought it was time for me to go home. I had a heart attack, with my right coronary artery ninety-nine percent blocked, and that is not one of the unimportant ones. I thought, *Lord, if this is it, I'm looking forward to heaven, but I really don't want my wife to be a widow.*

> "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me." (Philippians 4:13)

You may say, *You don't know my situation. If you knew what I was going through, you'd understand.* I have news for you: God knows exactly what you are going through. Better news: God understands. Even better: He has made a way to escape, that you may be able to bear it. The only question is whether you will take it.

A lot of veterans today feel that PTSD is beyond God's help. They might never say so out loud. But we are not the first soldiers who ever lived, and plenty came through their wars and out the other side. Many in the past journaled their way through life, and a lot of people with PTSD have found journaling one of the best things they can do. *Here is what happened. Here is how I responded. Here is how the Bible says I should have responded.* There is a way through.

> "According as his divine power hath given unto us all things that pertain unto life and godliness, through the knowledge of him that hath called us to glory and virtue:" (2 Peter 1:3)

That is either true or it isn't. If it is true, then the way to escape is among the "all things" He has given you.

## God sees the walking wounded, and He heals them

God sees your wound. He knows your name and your address. He knows exactly where you live.

> "Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you." (1 Peter 5:7)

Sometimes we don't cast our care on Him because, deep down, we doubt He really cares. That is where faith wavers. But listen:

> "The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit. Many are the afflictions of the righteous: but the LORD delivereth him out of them all." (Psalms 34:18-19)

*Nigh* means close at hand. You might say, *Well, He didn't deliver me out of it.* He made a way to escape, that you might be able to bear it.

> "He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds." (Psalms 147:3)

## God uses wounded people on purpose

Moses was wounded by failure. He set out to rescue his people, killed an Egyptian, ran for his life, and tended sheep in the wilderness for forty years. Then he came back and spent another forty years tending the flock of God in that same wilderness.

David was wounded by his own sin, and sometimes we have to admit that we are too.

Elijah was wounded by fear. He had just seen God deal with the prophets of Baal, hundreds of them, and send the rain, and then one woman threatened his life and he forgot all of it.

> "And when he saw that, he arose, and went for his life, and came to Beersheba, which belongeth to Judah, and left his servant there." (1 Kings 19:3)

Peter was wounded by his denial. I want to be careful here. I have never denied Christ. But have I denied the Holy Spirit's working in my life, when He said *witness to that person* and I said, in effect, *not today*? I have.

Paul was wounded by persecution, and he called it this:

> "For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory;" (2 Corinthians 4:17)
> "But we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us." (2 Corinthians 4:7)

Earthen vessels are fragile clay jars. That is what we are, formed from the dust of the earth. God chose weak, imperfect people on purpose so that His power would be seen and He would get the glory.

I have heard people say, *I think God is done with me. I'm tired. I'm just done.*

> "And the vessel that he made of clay was marred in the hand of the potter: so he made it again another vessel, as seemed good to the potter to make it." (Jeremiah 18:4)

Have you ever thought you've been marred? The potter didn't throw the clay out. He made it again. God remakes damaged vessels into something He can use, if we let Him. My wife and I have learned that God will let you know when He is finished with you. You don't tell God when you are finished with Him, and things will not go well if you try.

## Jacob limped and Paul kept his thorn

After Jacob had served Laban for years and been cheated ten times over, God spoke to him:

> "And the LORD said unto Jacob, Return unto the land of thy fathers, and to thy kindred; and I will be with thee." (Genesis 31:3)

Jacob had the promise. But before he could move forward, he had a night to get through.

> "And Jacob was left alone; and there wrestled a man with him until the breaking of the day." (Genesis 32:24)

By morning his name had changed:

> "And he said, Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Israel: for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed." (Genesis 32:28)

His character changed and his future changed. So did his walk.

> "And as he passed over Penuel the sun rose upon him, and he halted upon his thigh." (Genesis 32:31)

He became a walking wounded man, marked for life. If he ever wondered whether it would go away, it never did. Yet he was more useful to God afterward than before.

Then there is Paul, with a thorn in the flesh that God chose not to remove. Maybe you have your own version of it. We usually skip past *why* God gave it to him, but Paul tells us, and he says it twice:

> "And lest I should be exalted above measure through the abundance of the revelations, there was given to me a thorn in the flesh, the messenger of Satan to buffet me, lest I should be exalted above measure." (2 Corinthians 12:7)

You don't have to raise your hand, but maybe you know the tendency to get puffed up and think you're really something. Three times Paul asked for the thorn to be taken away. God's answer to him is much the same as His answer to you:

> "For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me. And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me." (2 Corinthians 12:8-9)

Grace is the ability to do with God what you cannot do by yourself. Sometimes grace *is* the way to escape. **The way to escape is often not the removal of the wound but the abundant grace of God enabling you to carry what He chooses not to remove.** The older you get, the more you realize it.

## A way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it

Jacob walked with a limp. Paul lived with a thorn. Neither one was ever made whole in this life, and both were more useful to God for it.

You are carrying something too. You may have been smiling over it for years. You may have let it harden into bitterness, or carried it home and shut the door. God sees it. He is near to the broken in heart, and He has already made a way for you to bear it.

So stop carrying it like a victim. Take it to Christ.

Victim or victor?

You choose.
