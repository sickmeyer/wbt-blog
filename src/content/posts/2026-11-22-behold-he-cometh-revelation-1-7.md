# Behold, He Cometh: Revelation 1:7 and the Return of Jesus Christ

I wear a little pin on my lapel. Two words: *Perhaps Today.*

It is my way of saying what Revelation 1:7 says. Jesus Christ is coming back, and it could be this very day. Some people see that pin and smile, the way you smile at a man who still believes something everybody else outgrew.

I understand why. Christians have been saying the Lord is coming for two thousand years. More than once in history, believers under horrific persecution were certain He would come in their lifetime. He did not. The end was not yet.

The Bible told us exactly what people would say about that:

> "Knowing this first, that there shall come in the last days scoffers, walking after their own lusts, And saying, Where is the promise of his coming? for since the fathers fell asleep, all things continue as they were from the beginning of the creation." (2 Peter 3:3-4)

Peter wrote that nearly two thousand years ago, and you could print it in tomorrow's paper. *Where is He? Nothing has changed.*

But John, writing near the end of that same century, set down a sentence that will not stay on the shelf:

> "Behold, he cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see him, and they also which pierced him: and all kindreds of the earth shall wail because of him. Even so, Amen." (Revelation 1:7)

Not *He might*. Not *someday, perhaps, in a manner of speaking*. **Behold, he cometh.** And every eye will see Him, including yours.

So the question is not whether. The question is whether you are ready.

## Where is the promise of his coming?

Some faith groups, even under the name of Christianity, do not take the return of Christ seriously. We do.

And we are certainly living in the days of the scoffers. This nation was founded as one nation under God, indivisible. Yet we are arguably more divided now than at any time in our history. The Lord said:

> "And Jesus knew their thoughts, and said unto them, Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation; and every city or house divided against itself shall not stand:" (Matthew 12:25)

I fear that is our country.

The world is spinning faster and faster toward chaos. There are wars and rumors of wars, and earthquakes in divers places. It is all happening, just as the Lord said it would:

> "And ye shall hear of wars and rumours of wars: see that ye be not troubled: for all these things must come to pass, but the end is not yet." (Matthew 24:6)

The disdain for Christianity keeps growing. Worse, people who are truly converted to Christ are caving into compromise. We are fast approaching the day the Lord Himself asked about:

> "I tell you that he will avenge them speedily. Nevertheless when the Son of man cometh, shall he find faith on the earth?" (Luke 18:8)

Back in the Dark Ages, under the persecution of the Inquisitions, believers were convinced the Pope was the Antichrist and the end had arrived. But the end was not yet.

It is coming, though. We do not know exactly when. But behold, He cometh.

## The next thing on God's calendar

According to the Bible, the next prophetic event is the rapture. It could happen literally at any moment.

That is not yet the moment Christ descends all the way to Jerusalem. At the rapture, we meet Him in the air:

> "For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first: Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord." (1 Thessalonians 4:16-17)

Every prophetic event up to that point has been fulfilled. That one is next.

I have always told people to watch the Middle East. Think about it. For the nations to come against Israel, even the United States will have to become a nation that stands against Israel. Whatever the situation, the nations will line up against her, and that is happening right now.

> "And when these things begin to come to pass, then look up, and lift up your heads; for your redemption draweth nigh." (Luke 21:28)

Things are moving lickety-split.

## Every eye shall see him, and on the very ground He left

The Bible is quite clear about where our Lord will return:

> "And when he had spoken these things, while they beheld, he was taken up; and a cloud received him out of their sight. And while they looked stedfastly toward heaven as he went up, behold, two men stood by them in white apparel; Which also said, Ye men of Galilee, why stand ye gazing up into heaven? this same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go into heaven. Then returned they unto Jerusalem from the mount called Olivet, which is from Jerusalem a sabbath day’s journey." (Acts 1:9-12)

He went up from the Mount of Olives in a cloud, and He will come down to the Mount of Olives in the clouds. Those clouds in Revelation speak of both the glory and the terror of His coming:

> "And to you who are troubled rest with us, when the Lord Jesus shall be revealed from heaven with his mighty angels, In flaming fire taking vengeance on them that know not God, and that obey not the gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ:" (2 Thessalonians 1:7-8)

Why Jerusalem? Jerusalem was once the faithful city. It is where the Lord met with His people in the temple. It is also where man rejected Him.

And why does He come at all? To fulfill all righteousness and to bring judgment upon the earth. It is about mankind being held to account:

> "And, behold, I come quickly; and my reward is with me, to give every man according as his work shall be. I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end, the first and the last." (Revelation 22:12-13)

He must judge all unrighteousness and sin, and He will judge equitably and justly.

## God is not slow. He is patient.

*But Pastor, they have been saying that for centuries. Maybe the scoffers have a point.*

The Bible answers that in the same chapter:

> "The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance." (2 Peter 3:9)

God is not just taking His time. There is purpose in everything He does, and He does not begin a thing He will not finish.

But His patience has an end. If you are lost and you have heard a clear presentation of the gospel, and the rapture takes place and you are left behind, you will not say, "Okay, I get it now." According to the Bible, it will be too late:

> "And for this cause God shall send them strong delusion, that they should believe a lie: That they all might be damned who believed not the truth, but had pleasure in unrighteousness." (2 Thessalonians 2:11-12)

So why do you and I need these reminders? Peter tells us:

> "Seeing then that all these things shall be dissolved, what manner of persons ought ye to be in all holy conversation and godliness," (2 Peter 3:11)

"Conversation" in the King James means your whole manner of life. We need the reminder so that we will order our lives in a more holy and more fruitful way, with less of self and more of God.

## In the twinkling of an eye

As a church, we believe in the literal, bodily, imminent return of Christ. *Imminent* means it could happen at any moment.

> "In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed." (1 Corinthians 15:52)

Do you know what a twinkling is? It is not a blink. A blink is fast, but a twinkling is far faster. A twinkling is the instant light first hits the eye.

That is how fast we will be gone. You could be standing there talking to someone, and you will disappear right before their eyes. You will be caught up with those who have gone before, and you will be changed into a glorified body.

Do you really believe all that? It is what the Bible says, and there is no reason to doubt it. Everything the Bible has said has come to pass. If you say, "Well, I just don't believe it," then you are choosing not to believe. That is all unbelief is: the refusal to believe.

> "For yourselves know perfectly that the day of the Lord so cometh as a thief in the night. For when they shall say, Peace and safety; then sudden destruction cometh upon them, as travail upon a woman with child; and they shall not escape." (1 Thessalonians 5:2-3)
> "But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only." (Matthew 24:36)

No one knows the day or the hour. But we know it will happen, because God said it would. And many of us, this preacher included, may be caught unaware.

## What comes after the trumpet

There is a study of the doctrine of last things. In the theological world it is called eschatology. Here is how the Bible lays it out.

The rapture is pre-tribulation. The second coming, when Christ comes all the way to the earth, comes at the end of the seven-year tribulation.

During that time the Antichrist will come on the scene, and at first Israel will believe he is their Messiah. A future temple is yet to be built. When I was in Israel, they showed us the priests' garments and the menorah that will stand in that temple, all ready. They say they have the Ark of the Covenant, though they did not show us that. They have even found the ashes of a red heifer for the sacrifices. All that is left is the building.

When the Antichrist goes into that temple and desecrates it, Israel will recognize him for what he is: an imposter, the devil incarnate.

We stood on Mount Megiddo, looking down over the Valley of Jezreel, which is also known as the Valley of Armageddon. In that valley Israel will be attacked by a two-hundred-million-man army. In that same valley God will come back and destroy that army, and the blood will rise to the height of a horse's bridle.

Those of us who went up in the rapture will come back with Him on white horses. I am so glad to be on that side, because He will make short work of the other:

> "And then shall that Wicked be revealed, whom the Lord shall consume with the spirit of his mouth, and shall destroy with the brightness of his coming:" (2 Thessalonians 2:8)

I know it sounds fantastic, too much to believe. The Lord knew that too:

> "If I have told you earthly things, and ye believe not, how shall ye believe, if I tell you of heavenly things?" (John 3:12)

## They also which pierced him

When He appears, there will be great wailing, especially among the Jews. They will realize that He really was who He said He was, the Messiah they so grievously rejected.

For those of us who are converted, a flood of emotion will already have come at the rapture. We will see Him in all His glory, shining like the sun. And I do not believe you and I will be able to get past the wounds in His hands and His feet.

Even in His glorified body, the holes will still be there, an eternal reminder of the price He paid for you and me to even be there.

> "And one shall say unto him, What are these wounds in thine hands? Then he shall answer, Those with which I was wounded in the house of my friends." (Zechariah 13:6)

Right now we serve Him by faith. We are made to walk by faith and not by sight, but we lean toward sight because that is how we relate to everything else. Still, if you have been born again, there is something in that new creature that gets excited at the thought of finally seeing Him.

I was listening to a trumpet solo of "Silent Night" just yesterday, and my mind went back to my dad. Dad was a trumpet player.

Every Christmas Eve he would sneak out to the farm where my grandparents lived. He would park at a distance, turn off the lights, and walk three or four hundred yards in the dark to the light pole in the front yard. Then he would point that trumpet at the stars and play. He played "It Came Upon the Midnight Clear" and "O Come, All Ye Faithful," and he always ended with "Silent Night."

About mid-song he would look over and see my grandparents standing in the doorway. Then they would switch on the light, and there would be hugging and tears, as you can imagine.

Nothing gets your attention like a trumpet. It is a call to attention.

When that trumpet sounds in heaven, it will not matter where you are. You could be riding the combine, sitting in a doctor's office, shopping for groceries, or cleaning house at home. We will be gone.

> "And it shall be said in that day, Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us: this is the LORD; we have waited for him, we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation." (Isaiah 25:9)

What a day, glorious day, that will be.

## Tears of joy, and the sadness of what we didn't do

For the lost, that day will bring regret, remorse, and remembrance. They will feel it profoundly.

For the saved, there will be tears of joy and deep gratitude. I believe there will also be some sadness for not having done more for Him in this life.

Listen to Paul:

> "Yea doubtless, and I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord: for whom I have suffered the loss of all things, and do count them but dung, that I may win Christ," (Philippians 3:8)
> "That I may know him, and the power of his resurrection, and the fellowship of his sufferings, being made conformable unto his death;" (Philippians 3:10)
> "Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended: but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before, I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus." (Philippians 3:13-14)

That is a surrendered life. Paul wanted to know Christ in this life as really as if he were already standing in heaven with Him. Nobody could argue that Paul was not sold out. Yet he was still a man, a sinner saved by grace, and he was frustrated with himself because there was always more ground to cover.

Paul always saw himself as inadequate for the call God had on his life. If there was ever anyone who wished he could go back and do some things differently, even as a saved man, I believe Paul would have put himself at the top of that list.

And you and I get so wrapped up in everyday life that we forget the simplest things:

> "Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded." (James 4:8)

## You must be converted

If you have not been converted, you are in absolute peril. You may not think it is that big of a deal. It is.

> "Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, when the times of refreshing shall come from the presence of the Lord;" (Acts 3:19)

You must be born again. There is only one way to receive a divine pardon, and that is through repentance and faith in the Lord Jesus Christ.

Repentance is not simply believing a set of facts. **Repentance is a complete change of mind and heart that results in a complete change of action and attitude.** It is a dying to self, turning from sin toward God, and trusting Christ as your Lord and personal Savior. At that moment you become a new creature. He is your Lord today.

There is a horrific teaching out there that I call easy prayerism. It says to admit you're a sinner, admit Christ died and rose again, pray this prayer, and you're going to heaven. No. It is not the prayer that saves. It is repentance and faith in Christ that save.

You can come to church your whole life and still split hell wide open. Church is a great thing, and I am glad you are reading this. But church will not save you.

An evangelist once got on an elevator with Bob Hope and asked him, "Mr. Hope, do you know for sure that if you died right now you would go to heaven?" Hope said, "Son, when you've given to as many charities as I have, you come and talk to me." You can give every charity millions of dollars, and it will not get you to heaven. You cannot buy heaven.

> "For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast." (Ephesians 2:8-9)

If you are saved, the Holy Spirit is living in you right now. That is real, and it is not to be taken for granted. He will never leave you, and you cannot lose what He has given:

> "And grieve not the holy Spirit of God, whereby ye are sealed unto the day of redemption." (Ephesians 4:30)

But being eternally secure is not a license to live like the devil. He is your Lord. He lives within you.

## Would you be ready if He came today?

Think about how you have lived the last month. We'll keep it conservative: just the last four weeks. If the Lord came today, would you be excited to see Him? Or would you say, "Oh, man..."?

*Well, Pastor, for the most part I'd be okay.*

Good. What's the other part?

*Well, if there were anything I could go back and do differently, I'd do it.*

Then start now, and go forward doing it differently. Yesterday is a canceled check. Tomorrow is a promissory note. **Today is the only cash you have, so spend it wisely.**

We make big plans for tomorrow. This Thursday it's turkey, ham, mashed potatoes, gravy, and all the trimmings. We thank the Lord for the bounty, and then we complain about the bounty after we've eaten it. *Get me a wheelbarrow and wheel me out.* We're a funny bunch that way.

We do have much to be thankful for. But are you ready to meet Him? Seriously?

Here is something you can do today. Stop and ask the Lord, "What could I be doing differently?" The funny thing is, you already know the answer. The Holy Spirit has been telling you for some time.

And if you say, "Honestly, Pastor, I'm doing everything right, and I can't see anything to improve," then you are full of yourself, and I would not mind telling you so. It's like that old song: *Oh Lord, it's hard to be humble when you're perfect in every way.* If you cannot see your need for improvement, you have not arrived. I need more of God now than I ever did.

A truth like that hurts. But it hurts good, because I know who the answer is, and I know where He is.

A governor of this state once said Christianity is for the simple-minded. Well, I'm simple-minded, and I'm glad I'm a Christian. A brother I know says he's working on a doctorate in simplicity. Sign me up.

When John finally saw the risen Christ on Patmos, here is what happened:

> "And when I saw him, I fell at his feet as dead. And he laid his right hand upon me, saying unto me, Fear not; I am the first and the last:" (Revelation 1:17)

Do not wait until the judgment day to get right with God.

## Behold, he cometh, and the time for excuses is over

> "Can two walk together, except they be agreed?" (Amos 3:3)

If you will not respond to the prodding of the Holy Spirit now, consider what the people left behind will be crying out in the day of His wrath:

> "And said to the mountains and rocks, Fall on us, and hide us from the face of him that sitteth on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lamb:" (Revelation 6:16)

If you die lost, with your sins unforgiven, you will never get another opportunity to repent. Every knee shall bow. This is not a time for excuses. It is a time for repentance, whether from sin if you are lost, or from fleshly living if you are saved.

> "Therefore thus will I do unto thee, O Israel: and because I will do this unto thee, prepare to meet thy God, O Israel." (Amos 4:12)

The scoffers are still asking where the promise of His coming is. You have the answer in your hands. Behold, He cometh with clouds, and every eye shall see Him.

I still wear the pin. Perhaps today.

Are you ready?
