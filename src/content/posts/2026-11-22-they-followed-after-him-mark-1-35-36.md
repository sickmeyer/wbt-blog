# They Followed After Him: Mark 1:35-36 and the Morning After Sunday

It had been a long night in Capernaum.

The sun went down, and the whole city came to one door. They brought the sick. They brought the ones possessed with devils. And Jesus healed them, one after another, well into the dark.

Then this, from Mark 1:35-36:

> "And in the morning, rising up a great while before day, he went out, and departed into a solitary place, and there prayed. And Simon and they that were with him followed after him." (Mark 1:35-36)

Read that second verse again. The miracles were over. The crowd had gone home. It was dark, they were worn out, and nobody would have blamed them for sleeping in.

**They followed after him anyway.**

Here is the question that verse puts to you and me. When the hype is gone, when Sunday is past, when the meeting is over and the excitement has worn off, will you still follow after Christ?

## The night before the morning

Go back a few verses and you can see what kind of night it was.

> "And at even, when the sun did set, they brought unto him all that were diseased, and them that were possessed with devils. And all the city was gathered together at the door. And he healed many that were sick of divers diseases, and cast out many devils; and suffered not the devils to speak, because they knew him." (Mark 1:32-34)

Earlier that day, on the Sabbath, He had taught in the synagogue and cast out a devil. Then came the evening. *All the city* was at the door, and He healed *many*.

I have a suspicion that Christ was up quite late that night. That was not a short meeting. And it was not only physical energy being spent. Ministry like that pulls on you emotionally and spiritually too. The disciples were there for all of it.

And the very next morning, before the sun came up, Jesus was already out the door to pray. Simon and the rest of them got up and went after Him. Which means they had to rise up early too.

It's wonderful to be in church, and you ought to be there. God can deal with your heart in a service. There can be that high, that refreshing, that sense of watching God work. But none of that means you get to neglect your walk with Him the next day.

**Late-night miracles are exciting, but don't forget the early-morning meditations.**

So let me ask you plainly. Did you come for the fame and have no fortitude once the fame was gone? Is church fun, but home is harder, and when the service ends your following ends with it?

Here is what I want you to take from this passage. Your worship does not have to pause between services. It shouldn't. The disciples did not have to wait until the next Sabbath to see Jesus work again. They chose to get up and follow Him on an ordinary morning.

I think of the meeting we just had, and how God spoke to so many hearts. That was a wonderful week. But now it's over. Am I still going to follow? Am I willing to say, "Lord, if I have to make a sacrifice, if I have to get up early, if I have to do something my flesh doesn't like, I'll do it, because I want to follow after You"?

There are four things in this passage that will help you follow after Christ.

## 1. A fervent devotion: "in the morning"

It's not easy to get up early after a late night. Nobody does it unless they have a reason.

We get up early for our jobs. We get up early when we're leaving on a trip we've looked forward to for months. Nobody has to drag us out of bed for that.

So how often do we get that excited about getting up to serve God?

I'll be the first to admit it isn't always easy. There are nights you are up late serving here at church, or taking care of things that had to be done. The next morning it's hard to get your heart right and be eager to serve. It would be dishonest of me to tell you it's easy.

That is exactly why it takes devotion. If your heart is right, it will still be hard, and you'll do it anyway, because you love Him.

Look at Job.

> "And his sons went and feasted in their houses, every one his day; and sent and called for their three sisters to eat and to drink with them. And it was so, when the days of their feasting were gone about, that Job sent and sanctified them, and rose up early in the morning, and offered burnt offerings according to the number of them all: for Job said, It may be that my sons have sinned, and cursed God in their hearts. Thus did Job continually." (Job 1:4-5)

What amazes me is the phrase *it may be*. Job didn't know his children had sinned. He wasn't putting out a fire. He rose up early just in case.

You men know how this works. Sometimes you walk into the store and pick up flowers for your wife, and there's no occasion. It isn't her birthday and it isn't your anniversary. You do it just because you want to.

How often do you come before God just because? Not because you need something, and not because you know there's a problem. You come because you want to spend time with Him and you want Him to know He matters to you.

Then there's Abraham, and this one is harder.

> "And it came to pass after these things, that God did tempt Abraham, and said unto him, Abraham: and he said, Behold, here I am. And he said, Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, and get thee into the land of Moriah; and offer him there for a burnt offering upon one of the mountains which I will tell thee of. And Abraham rose up early in the morning, and saddled his ass, and took two of his young men with him, and Isaac his son, and clave the wood for the burnt offering, and rose up, and went unto the place of which God had told him." (Genesis 22:1-3)

The proof of Abraham's obedience is that he went. But the proof of his faith and his devotion is that he *rose up early* to go.

It's one thing to obey. It's another thing to refuse to delay.

I don't think there's a single one of us who would be in a hurry if God asked something like that of us. We would find reasons to wait. Abraham didn't, because he knew God would not fail.

Put Job and Abraham side by side and you see this: **if your day doesn't begin with God when it's easy, it won't begin with Him when it's hard either.** Job rose early when life was going well, and that habit carried him when everything fell apart.

Your enthusiasm for God after Sunday has passed reveals your real passion for Him, or the lack of it. If it falls off as soon as the excitement fades, the passion was never really there. If it's real, it won't fade between services.

## 2. A faithful private worship: "a solitary place"

Jesus "went out, and departed into a solitary place." There came a point when He went to be alone with His Father.

It is good for you to be in church. It's necessary. God works through the preaching of His Word. But church does not replace your daily time alone with Him. If anything, you should take home what you heard here and let it make your time with Him richer.

I'll be honest with you. I can see who is in church. I can't see what your walk with God is like when you're at home and alone. That's between you and Him.

God wants to meet you alone, where there's nobody else to blame and nobody else to lean on. Look at Jacob.

> "And Jacob was left alone; and there wrestled a man with him until the breaking of the day. And when he saw that he prevailed not against him, he touched the hollow of his thigh; and the hollow of Jacob’s thigh was out of joint, as he wrestled with him. And he said, Let me go, for the day breaketh. And he said, I will not let thee go, except thou bless me. And he said unto him, What is thy name? And he said, Jacob. And he said, Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Israel: for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed." (Genesis 32:24-28)

*Jacob was left alone.* That night changed his name and his life. If you neglect your time alone with God, you can miss moments like that.

None of this makes public worship any less important. Public worship and private worship go together, and one does not replace the other. You can't say, "I can worship God anywhere, so I don't need church." That is exactly what Scripture warns against:

> "Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching." (Hebrews 10:25)

You also can't say, "I'm in church, so I get all I need there and don't need a daily quiet time." That's wrong too. God wants every part of your heart and every part of your life.

Here is something amazing. God deals with people one at a time even in a crowded room. One man preaches to many people, and the Holy Spirit speaks to each heart differently, according to what is going on in that life. He is that good. But you can't hide behind everyone else, and He still wants to meet with you alone, every day.

So do you have that time with Him every day?

## 3. First to prayer: "and there prayed"

He departed into a solitary place, "and there prayed."

If prayer is a priority, putting God first even when it costs your flesh something, you will receive far more of what God has for you.

**You should never take a vacation from prayer. But you most certainly can, and should, take a vacation to pray.**

My wife and I like to go camping. When we're out there, part of my flesh just wants to be lazy and do nothing, and that is more or less the point of a vacation. But I have to remind myself that I'm not there to get so lazy that I forget about God. I can take a break from the world. I never take a break from Him.

That's exactly what Jesus did. The day before, He was teaching in the synagogue and working miracles. When He stepped away, He didn't rest from His Father. He used the time alone to pray.

So when does God hear your voice?

> "Give ear to my words, O LORD, consider my meditation. Hearken unto the voice of my cry, my King, and my God: for unto thee will I pray. My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee, and will look up." (Psalms 5:1-3)

I don't know when your day starts. Some of you work nights. But when your day starts, does God hear your voice? It's easy to wake up already buried in everything that has to get done. Your first thought ought to be prayer. If that means getting up a little earlier and giving up a little comfort, it will be worth it. It will always be worth it.

Does God hear from you only when it's convenient?

Think about Daniel. The men who hated him went to the king:

> "All the presidents of the kingdom, the governors, and the princes, the counsellors, and the captains, have consulted together to establish a royal statute, and to make a firm decree, that whosoever shall ask a petition of any God or man for thirty days, save of thee, O king, he shall be cast into the den of lions." (Daniel 6:7)

The king signed it. And then:

> "Now when Daniel knew that the writing was signed, he went into his house; and his windows being open in his chamber toward Jerusalem, he kneeled upon his knees three times a day, and prayed, and gave thanks before his God, as he did aforetime." (Daniel 6:10)

The hard reality is that the times when it's least convenient to pray are often the times you need it most. For Daniel, praying meant a death sentence. That was exactly when he needed prayer most.

I don't know what would have happened if Daniel had stopped praying, or tried to pray in secret. I'm not going to claim I do. But he was not afraid to go before God, and God shut the lions' mouths.

Most of us aren't facing lions. We're facing busy schedules and small inconveniences. But here is something to think about. **The moment you decide God is too inconvenient for you, don't be surprised if He treats you the same way.**

The disciples got up in the dark after a late night. That wasn't convenient. They knew it was worth it.

## 4. Fast to follow: "followed after him"

"And Simon and they that were with him followed after him."

When God is dealing with your heart and He moves, you need to respond right away. If you delay, you may lose what He had for you. You may miss where He went.

I am not talking about salvation. A lost soul who calls on the Lord will be saved, and that door stays open.

> "The LORD is nigh unto all them that call upon him, to all that call upon him in truth." (Psalms 145:18)

But a saved person can miss a great many blessings by not responding when he should.

The children of Israel learned to live this way in the wilderness. Their whole camp moved when God moved:

> "And when the cloud was taken up from the tabernacle, then after that the children of Israel journeyed: and in the place where the cloud abode, there the children of Israel pitched their tents." (Numbers 9:17)

When the cloud lifted, they went. They didn't wait for a better time or a more comfortable morning. If they had stayed behind, they would have woken up to an empty sky and a God who had already gone on.

## Will you follow after Him when Sunday is over?

Go back to that dark morning in Capernaum.

The crowds are gone. The miracles are over. Nobody is gathered at the door anymore. Jesus is already out on a hillside somewhere, praying.

And Simon has a choice. He can roll over and tell himself he's earned the rest, after the night they all just had. Or he can get up in the dark and go find his Lord.

You have the same choice tomorrow morning, and the morning after that. The meeting is over. The excitement has faded. Your worship doesn't have to fade with it.

Will you rise up early?

Will you find your solitary place?

Will you follow after Him?
